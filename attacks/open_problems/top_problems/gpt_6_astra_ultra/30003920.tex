\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A null cone is a hypersurface generated by null geodesics; its spacelike two-surface sections carry null expansions. Hawking energy is a quasilocal mass constructed from a section\textquotesingle s area and the integral of the product of its two null expansions, using a fixed normalization. Trautman--Bondi mass is the total mass at a cut of future null infinity. A null Penrose inequality compares an appropriate initial horizon or section area with an asymptotic mass along a null hypersurface.

\paragraph{A round-cone normalization check.} In a spherical exterior take outgoing and ingoing null normals normalized to inner product \(-2\), with expansions \(\theta_+=2/r\), \(\theta_-=-2f(r)/r\). For round sections of area \(A=4\pi r^2\), define
\[
 m_H=\sqrt{A/(16\pi)}\left(1+\frac1{16\pi}\int\theta_+\theta_-\,dA\right).
\]
Since the product is constant on the sphere, substitution gives \(m_H=r(1-f(r))/2\). For \(f(r)=1-2M/r\), the result is exactly \(M\) at every radius. At the round horizon \(r=2M\), it equals \(\sqrt{A/(16\pi)}\), so the expected Penrose relation is an equality in this benchmark.
\paragraph{The cancellation requiring control.} For \(f(r)=1-2M/r+o(r^{-1})\), the same formula gives \(m_H\to M\). Merely assuming \(f(r)=1+o(1)\) would not suffice: \(f=1-r^{-1/2}\) gives \(m_H=\sqrt r/2\). This latter lapse is an asymptotic algebraic countercheck, not an asserted admissible vacuum cone.

A limit alone also gives no monotonic comparison to the initial area. Proving those geometric estimates and monotonicity under the source hypotheses is the first gap.
