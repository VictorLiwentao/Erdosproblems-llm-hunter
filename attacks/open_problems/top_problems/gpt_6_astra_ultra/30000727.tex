{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30000727 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} Over $\mathbb C$, a fusion category is a finite semisimple rigid linear tensor category with simple tensor unit and finite-dimensional morphism spaces. Its rank is the number of isomorphism classes of simple objects. A ribbon category in this question is a fusion category equipped with the compatible braiding, duality, and twist structure. Tensor equivalence identifies categories by a linear monoidal equivalence, with the relevant additional structures respected for the ribbon version.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The fixed-rank parameter ranges over positive integers. The report distinguishes the characteristic-zero setting from positive characteristic, and its rank-two and ribbon-rank-three results are special cases rather than proof of the full conjecture.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For each fixed rank, are there only finitely many fusion categories, and likewise only finitely many ribbon categories, up to tensor equivalence?

\paragraph{Saved literature context.} The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The Wang--Ostrik finiteness conjecture for all fusion categories of a fixed rank remains open. Rank-finiteness is known for important subclasses such as modular and braided fusion categories. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

\paragraph{Testing whether rank bounds the fusion coefficients.} For every integer $n\geq0$, consider the free abelian group with basis $1,x$ and multiplication
\[
 1x=x1=x,\qquad x^2=1+nx,\qquad x^*=x.
\]
It is an associative based ring: it is the quotient $\mathbb Z[t]/(t^2-nt-1)$ with the displayed basis, all structure constants are nonnegative, and the coefficient of $1$ in $xx^*$ is one. Its positive dimension assignment sends
\[
 x\longmapsto d_n=\frac{n+\sqrt{n^2+4}}2,
\]
since $d_n^2=1+nd_n$. Thus even rank two allows unbounded candidate fusion coefficients at the level of these elementary ring axioms.

\paragraph{Why categorification is the essential missing input.} A fusion category would additionally need associativity isomorphisms satisfying pentagon equations, duality data and, for the ribbon question, braiding and twist constraints. The constructed rings are not asserted to admit any of those structures. For $n=0$ the ring is the group ring of the two-element group, but this endpoint does not authorize the same conclusion for arbitrary $n$.

\paragraph{Attempt outcome.} The naive enumeration route ``fixed rank gives finitely many nonnegative multiplication tables'' is false before categorical constraints are used: the tables above already form an infinite sequence. Therefore a proof of the source conjecture must bound categorifiable coefficients using substantially more than positivity, rigidity-style coefficient identities and the rank. Neither a bound on actual categories nor finiteness of compatible ribbon structures is established. This is consistent with the source's separate known low-rank results and does not challenge their classification.
