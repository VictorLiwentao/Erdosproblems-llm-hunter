\begin{proposition}
Agreement at $d+1$ distinct rational inputs certifies equality of two such polynomials. Any fixed set of at most $d$ test inputs can accept an unequal polynomial pair. Cancelling rational expressions without tracking domains is a separate obstruction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The difference of two accepted polynomials has degree at most $d$. A nonzero degree-$d$ polynomial has at most $d$ distinct roots: a root $a$ gives a factor $X-a$ by polynomial division, and repeated division at distinct roots decreases the degree each time. Thus $d+1$ agreements force zero difference. Conversely, for fixed test inputs $a_1,\ldots,a_s$ with $s\le d$, the polynomials zero and $\prod_{j=1}^s(X-a_j)$ agree at every test point but differ identically. Repeated test inputs only reduce the information. For $s=0$, the empty product is one.

Finally, $X/X$ and 1 have equal values at every nonzero input, but their natural real domains differ at zero. Reducing the first expression to 1 without preserving its excluded input therefore changes the represented partial function, even if numerator/denominator cancellation is algebraically correct.
\end{proof}
