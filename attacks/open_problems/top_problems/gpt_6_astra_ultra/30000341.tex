\section{Bounded Local Observables in Factorizing Quantum Field Theories}\label{30000341}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30000341 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} In two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime a wedge is an unbounded causal region and a double cone is the bounded intersection of a future and a past light cone. The report constructs a wedge-local net $W\mapsto A(W)$ on a Hilbert space from a factorizing two-particle scattering matrix $S$. For an operator algebra $A$, its commutant is $A'=\{T:TX=XT\ \forall X\in A\}$. If $W_1\subset W_2$ and $O=W_1'\cap W_2$ is a double cone, the candidate local algebra is $A(O)=A(W_1)'\cap A(W_2)$. Nontrivial means $A(O)\ne\mathbb C I$.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The target is the report's admissible factorizing S-matrix family and wedge construction. Its modular-nuclearity subfamily already has nontrivial double-cone observables; those positive cases do not settle every admissible S-matrix.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For the wedge-local net associated with a factorizing two-dimensional S-matrix, do nontrivial observables localized in bounded double cones exist? Equivalently, for nested wedges $W_1\subset W_2$, is the double-cone algebra $A(W_1)'\cap A(W_2)$ nontrivial?

The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

\paragraph{Testing nontriviality through split inclusions.} An elementary operator-algebra mechanism would work if the wedge inclusion admitted a spatial tensor representation
\[
 A(W_1)\subset B(\mathcal K)\otimes I\subset A(W_2)\subset B(\mathcal K\otimes\mathcal L),
\]
and if $A(W_2)$ also contained $I\otimes B(\mathcal L)$ with $\dim\mathcal L>1$. Under these explicit hypotheses $I\otimes B(\mathcal L)\subset A(W_1)'\cap A(W_2)$, and any nontrivial projection on $\mathcal L$ gives a bounded nonscalar local observable. Commutation is immediate on elementary tensors and extends by continuity.

\paragraph{Why an intermediate type-I factor alone is insufficient.} Set $\mathcal K=\mathbb C^2$, $\mathcal L=\mathbb C^2$, and $A(W_1)=A(W_2)=B(\mathcal K)\otimes I$. There is a type-I factor between the two algebras, but the relative commutant inside $A(W_2)$ is only $\mathbb C I$. Indeed writing an element as $X\otimes I$, commutation with all matrix units forces $X$ scalar. The preceding sufficient criterion specifically needs independent operators in the larger algebra, not just an ambient tensor decomposition.

\paragraph{Attempt outcome.} This exposes the first missing implication in a bare ``split therefore nontrivial'' argument. The source's modular nuclearity method has additional vacuum and standardness information; none of it is proved by the toy inclusion. To treat the full factorizing-S-matrix family one must obtain those structural hypotheses from the actual wedge representation, or construct a nonscalar element directly. No result for an S-matrix outside the already known subfamily is established here.
