\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}For compact $K$, its approximation number is $\alpha_N(K)=\inf_{\operatorname{rank}F<N}\|K-F\|$. Write $\operatorname{spr}(A)=\sup\{|z|:z\in\sigma(A)\}$. Resonances are poles of a suitably continued resolvent; an isoresonant family has the same resonance data. The source uses a J-self-adjoint structure and refers to J as a conjugation; its exact convention must be preserved in a chosen approximation scheme.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
The record bundles source items 17.1--19.1.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Devise projected-space methods for rough rigorous and sharp useful enclosures of spectra of J-self-adjoint operators, incorporating the source’s J structure, and assess consequences for their evolution problems.
\item For Schrödinger operators $-\Delta+V$ on $\mathbb R^d$ with bounded compactly supported potentials, find spectral or pseudospectral data that distinguish members of isoresonant complex-potential families in dimensions $d\ge2$, including nontrivial smooth complex potentials with no resonances. Ask the analogous question for isospectral families in settings such as the circle.
\item For a Banach-space operator $A$ and compact perturbation $K$ with $\alpha_N(K)\to0$, estimate discrete eigenvalue counts of $A+K$ outside disks using resolvent growth near $\sigma(A)$. In particular classify operators whose resolvent has polynomial growth toward the spectrum; classify those admitting $M\ge1$ such that $\|(\lambda-A)^{-1}\|\le M/\operatorname{dist}(\lambda,\sigma(A))$ for every resolvent parameter; and classify those admitting $\|(\lambda-A)^{-1}\|\le M/(|\lambda|-\operatorname{spr}(A))$ whenever $|\lambda|>\operatorname{spr}(A)$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{A projected-space exclusion certificate.}Let a bounded operator on an orthogonal sum be \(T=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}\), with the first block finite dimensional. If \(D-z\) is invertible, block elimination proves that \(T-z\) is invertible exactly when \(S(z)=A-z-B(D-z)^{-1}C\) is invertible. If \(A-z\) is invertible and
\[\|(A-z)^{-1}\|\,\|B\|\,\|(D-z)^{-1}\|\,\|C\|<1,
\]
then factoring \(S=(A-z)[I-(A-z)^{-1}B(D-z)^{-1}C]\) and summing a Neumann series certifies exclusion of \(z\) from the spectrum. A finite compression by itself omits the decisive complement resolvent.
\paragraph{A finite-dimensional classification test.}For a complex matrix, a bound \(\|(T-z)^{-1}\|\le M/\operatorname{dist}(z,\sigma(T))\) for every resolvent parameter holds if and only if the matrix is diagonalizable. Sufficiency follows from \(T=S\Lambda S^{-1}\), with \(M=\|S\|\|S^{-1}\|\). For necessity, a Jordan block of size \(r\ge2\) has resolvent term proportional to \((z-\lambda)^{-r}\); applying it to the last basis vector gives a norm bounded below by a positive constant times that power near \(\lambda\), even after fixed invertible similarity. This contradicts a reciprocal-distance bound.
\paragraph{Gap and conventions.}Neither argument uses or changes the source's antilinear conjugation \(J\). A useful \(J\)-compatible projection scheme must establish complement estimates respecting the actual structure. Infinite-dimensional polynomial resolvent classifications, compact-perturbation counting, and data distinguishing isoresonant or isospectral potentials do not follow from the finite-matrix test. All remain unresolved, including the exterior-disk classification, which is different from distance-to-spectrum control.
