\subsection{Attempt: whether exam scores contain the intended information}
The record's reconstruction is unverified and gives no grading or alignment model. I tested an explicit linear version: a learner has a skill vector $k\in[0,1]^d$, the recorded exam scores are $Ak$, and a desired content summary is $t^{\mathsf T}k$. The question is whether the latter can be recovered exactly for all skill profiles from the recorded scores.
\begin{proposition}
Exact recovery is possible if and only if $t^{\mathsf T}$ lies in the row space of $A$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $t^{\mathsf T}=c^{\mathsf T}A$, apply the linear rule $c^{\mathsf T}$ to the recorded score vector to recover the target. Conversely, decompose $t=u+v$ into its orthogonal projections onto the row space of $A$ and its orthogonal complement. If $t$ is not in that row space, then $v\ne0$, $Av=0$, and $t^{\mathsf T}v=\|v\|^2>0$. For a sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$, both
\[
k_+=\tfrac12\mathbf1+\epsilon v,\qquad k_-=\tfrac12\mathbf1-\epsilon v
\]
belong to $[0,1]^d$. Their recorded scores agree because $Av=0$, but their target summaries differ by $2\epsilon\|v\|^2$. No function of the recorded score can assign both required answers. This proves necessity even allowing nonlinear recovery rules.
\end{proof}
