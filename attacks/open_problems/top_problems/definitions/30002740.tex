\section{Quasicrystalline Gibbs Phases in Higher Dimensions}\label{30002740}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30002740 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A short-range lattice interaction has local or suitably decaying couplings as specified by its Gibbs specification. A translation-invariant Gibbs measure is invariant under all lattice shifts. Its extremal decomposition expresses it as a mixture of pure Gibbs phases; the pure phases in that decomposition need not individually share the mixture\textquotesingle s translation invariance. Nonperiodic here excludes invariance under any finite-index translation subgroup.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
In every dimension at least $3$, does there exist a translation-invariant Gibbs measure for a short-range interaction whose extremal decomposition contains nonperiodic Gibbs measures?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The quantifier over dimensions and the distinction between a translation-invariant mixture and its nonperiodic extremal components are part of the target. The state space and meaning of short range remain source-specific.
\subsection{Short English statement}
In each dimension at least three, can a translation-invariant equilibrium state be a mixture containing nonperiodic pure phases?
