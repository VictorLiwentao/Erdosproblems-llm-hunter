\section{Are there infinitely many Pell primes}\label{9400156}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400156 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


The Pell numbers are $P_0=0$, $P_1=1$ and $P_{n+2}=2P_{n+1}+P_n$ for $n\ge0$. A Pell prime is a prime value $P_n$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many $n\ge0$ such that $P_n$ is prime? \sref{9400156}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do infinitely many Pell numbers take prime values?
