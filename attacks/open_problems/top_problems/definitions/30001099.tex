\section{Capacity-Preserving Conversion to Unsplittable Flows}\label{30001099}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 30001099 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.


A splittable flow may route one demand along several paths; an unsplittable flow routes its entire demand on one path. $d_{\max}=\max_i d_i$, $x_e,y_e$ are edge loads, and $c(x)=\sum_e c_e x_e$.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Given a single-source network with capacities $u$, costs $c$, source $s$, sinks $t_i$, and demands $d_i$ satisfying the no-bottleneck condition $d_{\max}\leq u_e$, does every splittable flow $x$ satisfying the demands admit an unsplittable flow $y$ satisfying the same demands such that $y_e\leq x_e+d_{\max}$ for every edge $e$ and $c(y)\leq c(x)$? \sref{30001099}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Round a single-source flow to one path per demand while preserving cost and allowing only the stated additive edge-load increase.
