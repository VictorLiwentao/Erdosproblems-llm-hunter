\section{Which integers can be written as the sum of three perfect cubes}\label{9400111}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400111 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $\mathcal C=\{x^3+y^3+z^3:x,y,z\in\mathbb Z\}$. Zero and negative cube summands are permitted. Since an integer cube is congruent to $0$, $1$ or $-1$ modulo $9$, no integer congruent to $4$ or $5$ modulo $9$ lies in $\mathcal C$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine $\mathcal C$. In particular, does every integer $N\not\equiv4,5\pmod9$ admit $N=x^3+y^3+z^3$ with $x,y,z\in\mathbb Z$, so that the displayed congruence obstruction is the complete characterization? \sref{9400111}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Which integers are sums of three integer cubes, allowing negative cubes and zero?
