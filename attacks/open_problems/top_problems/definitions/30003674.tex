\section{Quantum Optimal Transport for Mean-Field Dynamics}\label{30003674}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30003674 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A quantum state is a positive trace-one operator. A coupling of two quantum states is a bipartite state with those partial traces. Quantum optimal transport seeks a cost or variational principle on such states analogous to classical optimal transport. A Benamou--Brenier formula in classical transport minimizes a kinetic action over density and velocity curves subject to a continuity equation. The source pseudo-metric for mean-field comparison has its own cost operator and need not be a genuine metric with zero self-distance.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Can the pseudo-metric used for quantum mean-field dynamics be formulated through a quantum analogue of optimal transport, such as a Benamou-Brenier variational formula?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The exact pseudo-metric and admissible quantum continuity equation are source-defined; different modern quantum Wasserstein metrics are not automatically the same quantity.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Give the quantum mean-field comparison distance an optimal-transport formulation, ideally through a dynamic variational formula.
