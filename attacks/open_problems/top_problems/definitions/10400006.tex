{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 10400006 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A knot or link is a smooth oriented embedding of one or more circles in the oriented $3$-sphere, considered up to orientation-preserving ambient isotopy. Write $O$ for the unknot, which bounds an embedded disk. The Jones polynomial is normalized by $V_O(t)=1$ and
\[t^{-1}V_{L_+}(t)-tV_{L_-}(t)=(t^{1/2}-t^{-1/2})V_{L_0}(t),\]
where the three oriented links agree outside a ball and inside it have respectively a positive crossing, a negative crossing, and the oriented smoothing. For knots $V_K(t)\in\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Find a geometric relationship between Jones polynomials and counts of points of algebraic varieties over finite fields. For a variety $X$ defined over $\mathbb F_q$, such a count means $\#X(\mathbb F_q)$. Determine a meaningful construction of varieties or geometric counting expressions associated to a knot that explains its Jones polynomial. Allow appropriate signed or cohomological expressions when needed; the question does not prescribe one universally positive point-count formula. \sref{10400006}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find an algebraic-geometric interpretation of Jones polynomials through finite-field point counts or their appropriate geometric refinements.
