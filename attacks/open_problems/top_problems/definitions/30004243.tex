\section{Sharp Free Energy of the Two-Dimensional Heisenberg Ferromagnet}\label{30004243}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30004243 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
For a finite lattice region $\Lambda$, the free energy per site at inverse temperature $\beta>0$ is $-(\beta|\Lambda|)^{-1}\log\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H_\Lambda}$, with the thermodynamic limit defining $f^{2d}$. The Heisenberg ferromagnet has nearest-neighbor spin-$S$ interactions favoring alignment, with the report\textquotesingle s ground-energy normalization; $S$ is an allowed positive half-integer spin. The displayed asymptotics concern large $\beta S$. A matching lower bound must recover the leading coefficient and the requested asymptotic error strength.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For the two-dimensional quantum Heisenberg ferromagnet, the free energy obeys

$$f^{2d}(\beta,S)\leq-\frac\pi{24}S^{-1}\beta^{-2}\left(1-O\bigl((\beta S)^{-\kappa}\bigr)\right)$$

for every $\kappa<1/3$. Can one prove a matching lower bound?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The Hamiltonian normalization determines the coefficient $\pi/24$. It must not be changed by an unreported coupling rescaling. The meaning of matching, including the range of $\kappa$, remains the comparison asked in the report.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Prove the lower free-energy estimate needed to make the two-dimensional ferromagnet\textquotesingle s spin-wave prediction sharp.
