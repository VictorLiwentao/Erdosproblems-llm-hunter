\section{Ramsey's Theorem for Triples over a Weak Base}\label{7500216}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 7500216 · Logic}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $[\mathbb N]^3$ be the three-element subsets of $\mathbb N$. Write $\mathsf{RT}^3_2$ for: every colouring $c:[\mathbb N]^3\to\{0,1\}$ has an infinite $H\subseteq\mathbb N$ on which $c$ is constant. $\mathsf{RCA}_0^*$ has $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension, bounded ($\Sigma^0_0$) induction and total exponentiation; adding $\Sigma^0_1$ induction yields $\mathsf{RCA}_0$. $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ adds arithmetical comprehension to $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does the familiar equivalence of Ramsey's theorem for triples with $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ continue to hold over $\mathsf{RCA}_0^*$, that is, does this weaker base prove $\mathsf{RT}^3_2\leftrightarrow\mathsf{ACA}_0$? This records the historical comparison; the later source treats its weak-induction setting in detail. \sref{7500216}{3} \sref{7500216}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does reducing induction in the base theory preserve the usual equivalence between Ramsey's theorem for triples and arithmetical comprehension?
