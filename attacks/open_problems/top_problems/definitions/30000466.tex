\section{Higher-Dimensional Thermodynamic Limits}\label{30000466}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30000466 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} For finite regions $\Lambda\subset\mathbb Z^d$, the sigma model has spins in a noncompact target manifold with nonamenable global symmetry and Gibbs distributions specified by interactions and boundary conditions. A thermodynamic limit takes $\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb Z^d$ and controls expectations of suitable observables, limit states, and their dependence on the boundary conditions. Because the target volume is noncompact, a normalization or symmetry fixing is needed in the report's construction.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The report notes boundary-condition dependence even for invariant observables. Existence, normalization, convergence, and dependence are different aspects of rigorous control; the question does not assert a unique boundary-independent limit.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Establish rigorous control of the thermodynamic limit in spatial dimension greater than one for nonlinear sigma models with noncompact target spaces and nonamenable symmetry groups.

\paragraph{Saved literature context.} The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Rigorous thermodynamic-limit control is available for selected one-dimensional and special higher-dimensional models, but the broad more-than-one-dimensional problem stated in the report remains open. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Control infinite-volume limits of the report's noncompact sigma models in more than one spatial dimension.
