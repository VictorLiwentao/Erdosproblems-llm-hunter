\section{Metric-Tensor Scaling in Liouville Quantum Gravity}\label{30003287}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30003287 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A Liouville quantum gravity field $h$ is a random generalized function; its exponential is defined by regularization rather than by pointwise evaluation. The parameter $\gamma$ controls the field coupling. A random metric\textquotesingle s Hausdorff dimension $\beta$ refers to covering behavior measured in that metric. A formal tensor $e^{2\gamma h/\beta}(dx^2+dy^2)$ describes a proposed conformal scaling prescription, rather than an ordinary smooth Riemannian tensor.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For $\gamma$-Liouville quantum gravity, is the scaling-limit metric tensor associated with the field $h$ given formally by

$$e^{2\gamma h/\beta}(dx^2+dy^2),$$

where $\beta$ is the Hausdorff dimension of the Liouville metric?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The displayed tensor is expressly formal. Because $h$ is distribution-valued, this is not an equality of pointwise metric coefficients; a regularization and limiting distance construction are required. No particular formula for $\beta$ is added.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the Liouville quantum gravity metric have the proposed formal conformal scaling with its Hausdorff dimension?
