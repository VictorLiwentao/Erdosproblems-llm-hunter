\section{Counterexamples to Strongly Continuous Many-Fermion Dynamics}\label{30006191}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30006191 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


For a one-particle test function $f\in L^2(\mathbb R^d)$, $a(f)$ is a bounded fermionic annihilation operator. The source's strong continuity means norm continuity of the CAR-algebra automorphism group: $\|\tau_t(a(f))-a(f)\|\to0$ as $t\to0$. A counterexample must give a nonzero lower bound along a sequence of times approaching zero. Suitable states can witness such a bound through expectations or vector norms.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For the unregularized continuum fermion Hamiltonian, find an interaction $V$ and states for which the Heisenberg evolution of $a(f)$ fails to be strongly continuous.

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The primary report explicitly distinguishes operator-norm continuity from strong operator continuity. Self-adjointness of a represented Hamiltonian supplies the latter and does not automatically settle the former. Related record 30006190 asks about cutoff removal.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find an unregularized fermion interaction and states exhibiting the requested failure of strong continuity.
